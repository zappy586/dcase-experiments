One synthetic-fault recipe, four machines

We ran a single class-invariant recipe, with Stable Audio Open (SAO) synthetic faults and no real anomalies, on ToyCar, fan, gearboxEmu and bearingEmu. It clearly works on ToyCar only. One measurement lines up with which machines work: how close the generated sounds are to the real recordings.

1 · Recipe2 · Results3 · Realism4 · Takeaways

1The recipe (same for every machine)

Generator

SAO with a DreamBooth LoRA (rank 64, 3,000 steps) trained on the machine's 1,000 real normal clips (near-minus-far audio), plus a decoder fine-tuned on the same clips. One generic description template; the machine name is the only thing that changes.

Synthetic data

144 sets per machine. Each set is one normal and three faults generated from the same noise. The fault text is drawn at random from 300 generic fault descriptions (25 kinds × 4 severities × 3 phrasings) that name no machine parts.

Scoring

Fault direction = average of (synthetic fault − its own synthetic normal) in BEATs features. A real test clip's anomaly score is its projection onto that direction. Nothing is trained on real anomalies; test labels are used only to compute Ω.

Measured on 8 fixed sets at the end of training. On all 144 sets, the synthetic faults sit as far from the real clips as the synthetic normals (ToyCar 15.9× against 16.1×; fan 41.2× against 41.1×), while real anomalies sit only 1.1–2.9× from the real normals.

4Takeaways

1

Synthetic faults alone carry real fault information on ToyCar. Ω 0.633 with no real anomalies, above every control.

2

The same recipe does not carry over to the other machines. Gearbox is at chance, bearing at the level of the controls, and fan points the wrong way on source.

3

The machines where it works are the ones SAO learned well. ToyCar's generated sounds are 16× from the real ones; gearbox and fan are 34–44×.

DCASE 2026 Task 2 development set, near-minus-far audio. Official score Ω = harmonic mean of source AUC, target AUC and pAUC (FPR ≤ 0.1). Script: tools/multi_machine.py --machine <m> --name "<name>".
